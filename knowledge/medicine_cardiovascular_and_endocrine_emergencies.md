# General Medicine: Acute Coronary Syndromes, Diabetic Ketoacidosis, and Heart Failure

## Acute Coronary Syndromes (ACS): Classification, ECG Localization, and Management

Acute Coronary Syndromes encompass a clinical spectrum resulting from rupture or erosion of an atherosclerotic plaque with superimposed platelet-rich thrombus formation.

### 1. ACS Spectrum Definitions
- **ST-Elevation Myocardial Infarction (STEMI):** Complete, persistent occlusive coronary thrombus producing transmural myocardial necrosis. Defined on 12-lead ECG by new ST-segment elevation at the J-point in $\ge 2$ contiguous leads ($\ge 2.5$ mm in men $<40$ y, $\ge 2.0$ mm in men $\ge 40$ y, $\ge 1.5$ mm in women in leads V2–V3; or $\ge 1.0$ mm in other leads) or new Left Bundle Branch Block (LBBB). High-sensitivity cardiac Troponin I or T elevated.
- **Non-ST-Elevation Myocardial Infarction (NSTEMI):** Incomplete or transiently occlusive thrombus producing subendocardial myocardial necrosis. ECG shows ST-segment depression ($\ge 0.5$ mm), T-wave inversions, or non-specific changes, with positive cardiac troponins.
- **Unstable Angina (UA):** Myocardial ischemia at rest or with minimal exertion without objective biomarker evidence of myocardial necrosis (normal cardiac troponins).

### 2. ECG Infarct Localization and Culprit Coronary Arteries
- **Anterior Wall / Anteroseptal:** Leads V1, V2, V3, V4 $\rightarrow$ **Left Anterior Descending (LAD)** coronary artery ("widow-maker").
- **Anterolateral / Extensive Anterior:** Leads V1 to V6, I, aVL $\rightarrow$ Proximal LAD or Mainstem Left Coronary Artery.
- **Inferior Wall:** Leads II, III, aVF $\rightarrow$ **Right Coronary Artery (RCA)** in 85–90% (right dominant circulation) or Left Circumflex (LCx). *Crucial clinical caveat:* Check right-sided leads (V4R) to rule out Right Ventricular (RV) infarction; avoid nitrates and diuretics in RV infarct due to preload dependence.
- **Lateral Wall:** Leads I, aVL, V5, V6 $\rightarrow$ **Left Circumflex (LCx)** or diagonal branches of LAD.
- **Posterior Wall:** Prominent tall R waves ($R/S > 1$), horizontal ST depression, and upright T waves in V1–V3; confirm with ST elevation in posterior leads V7–V9 $\rightarrow$ Posterior Descending Artery (PDA from RCA or LCx).

### 3. Acute STEMI Stabilization and Reperfusion Benchmarks
- **Immediate Antiplatelet & Anticoagulant Therapy:**
  * Aspirin: 162–325 mg non-enteric chewable loading dose immediately (COX-1 inhibitor).
  * P2Y12 Receptor Inhibitor loading dose: Ticagrelor (180 mg) or Prasugrel (60 mg) preferred over Clopidogrel (600 mg).
  * Anticoagulation: Unfractionated heparin (weight-adjusted IV bolus and infusion) or Enoxaparin.
  * Nitrates (sublingual 0.4 mg) for ischemic pain relief (contraindicated in hypotension, RV infarct, or recent phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitor use).
  * High-intensity Statin: Atorvastatin 80 mg daily.
- **Reperfusion Strategy Timelines:**
  * **Primary Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI):** The gold standard. Target **Door-to-Balloon time $< 90$ minutes** at a PCI-capable center, or $< 120$ minutes from first medical contact if transfer is required.
  * **Intravenous Fibrinolysis:** Indicated if primary PCI cannot be accomplished within 120 minutes of medical contact and no contraindications exist (e.g., prior intracranial hemorrhage, active bleeding, ischemic stroke within 3 months). Target **Door-to-Needle time $< 30$ minutes** using fibrin-specific agents: Tenecteplase (TNK-tPA), Reteplase, or Alteplase.

## Guideline-Directed Medical Therapy (GDMT) for Heart Failure (HFrEF)

For Heart Failure with Reduced Ejection Fraction (LVEF $\le 40\%$), contemporary consensus guidelines establish **Four Foundational Medication Pillars** that reduce cardiovascular mortality and heart failure hospitalizations:

1. **Renin-Angiotensin System Inhibition:**
   * **Angiotensin Receptor-Neprilysin Inhibitor (ARNI - Sacubitril/Valsartan):** First-line preferred therapy. Sacubitril inhibits neprilysin, preventing degradation of natriuretic peptides (ANP, BNP, CNP) and bradykinin, promoting natriuresis, vasodilation, and antifibrotic effects; Valsartan blocks AT1 receptors. Requires a 36-hour washout period when switching from an ACE inhibitor to prevent severe angioedema.
   * *Alternative:* ACE Inhibitor (Enalapril, Ramipril, Lisinopril) or ARB (Candesartan, Losartan) if ARNI is unavailable or cost-prohibitive.
2. **Evidence-Based Beta-Blockers:**
   * Only three specific beta-blockers demonstrate proven mortality reduction in HFrEF: **Carvedilol** (non-selective $\beta_1/\beta_2$ with $\alpha_1$ blockade), **Metoprolol Succinate** (long-acting cardioselective $\beta_1$), and **Bisoprolol** ($\beta_1$ selective). Initiate at low doses in stable euvolemic patients and titrate gradually.
3. **Mineralocorticoid Receptor Antagonists (MRA):**
   * **Spironolactone** or **Eplerenone** (selective MRA without anti-androgenic side effects like gynecomastia). Competitively block aldosterone receptors in distal renal tubule and myocardium, reducing myocardial fibrosis. Monitor serum potassium ($K^+ < 5.0$ mEq/L) and eGFR ($> 30$ mL/min/1.73 $m^2$).
4. **Sodium-Glucose Cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) Inhibitors:**
   * **Dapagliflozin** (10 mg daily) or **Empagliflozin** (10 mg daily). Effective regardless of diabetes status; reduces intraglomerular pressure, promotes osmotic diuresis and ketogenesis, improves myocardial energetics, and mitigates adverse cardiac remodeling.
- **Symptomatic Decongestion:** Loop diuretics (Furosemide, Torsemide, Bumetanide) titrated to maintain euvolemia, but they do not confer independent long-term mortality benefits.

## Diabetic Emergencies: Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA) versus Hyperosmolar Hyperglycemic State (HHS)

Both DKA and HHS are life-threatening acute complications of decompensated diabetes mellitus precipitated by infections (pneumonia, UTI), non-adherence to insulin, myocardial infarction, or acute illness.

| Clinical & Biochemical Parameter | Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA) | Hyperosmolar Hyperglycemic State (HHS) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Typical Patient Profile** | Type 1 Diabetes (can occur in Type 2 under severe stress or with SGLT2i) | Type 2 Diabetes (often elderly with impaired thirst mechanism or institutionalized) |
| **Onset Speed** | Rapid, acute ($< 24$ hours) | Insidious, over days to weeks (gradual polyuria, dehydration) |
| **Plasma Glucose Level** | Usually $250\text{ to }600\text{ mg/dL}$ ($13.9\text{ to }33.3\text{ mmol/L}$) | Markedly elevated: $> 600\text{ mg/dL}$ ($> 33.3\text{ mmol/L}$), often $> 1000\text{ mg/dL}$ |
| **Arterial Blood pH** | Acidemic: $< 7.30$ (Severe: $< 7.00$) | Normal or mildly reduced: $> 7.30$ |
| **Serum Bicarbonate ($HCO_3^-$)** | Low: $< 18\text{ mEq/L}$ (Severe: $< 10\text{ mEq/L}$) | Normal: $> 18\text{ mEq/L}$ |
| **Serum / Urine Ketones** | Strongly positive ($\beta$-hydroxybutyrate $\gg$ acetoacetate) | Absent or small/trace |
| **Effective Serum Osmolality** | Variable, typically $< 320\text{ mOsm/kg}$ | Markedly hyperosmolar: $> 320\text{ mOsm/kg}$ |
| **Serum Anion Gap** | Elevated: High Anion Gap Metabolic Acidosis ($> 12\text{ mEq/L}$) | Usually normal ($< 12\text{ mEq/L}$) |
| **Clinical Hallmarks** | Kussmaul respirations (deep, rapid breathing), acetone/fruity breath odor, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain | Profound dehydration ($8\text{ to }12\text{ L}$ fluid deficit), altered mental status, lethargy, stupor, coma, focal neurologic deficits |

### Structured Emergency Protocol for DKA Management
1. **Intravenous Fluid Resuscitation:** Initial fluid of choice is **0.9% Normal Saline (NaCl)** at $1000\text{ to }1500\text{ mL/hr}$ in the first hour to restore effective circulating volume and renal perfusion. Switch to 0.45% NaCl if corrected serum sodium is normal or elevated. When blood glucose falls below $200\text{ to }250\text{ mg/dL}$, add **5% Dextrose (D5W)** to IVF to prevent hypoglycemia while continuing insulin to clear ketoacidosis.
2. **Potassium Repletion (The Golden Rule):** Insulin drives potassium into cells, risking fatal cardiac arrhythmias.
   * If serum $K^+ < 3.3\text{ mEq/L}$: **HOLD insulin**; administer IV KCl ($20\text{ to }30\text{ mEq/hr}$) until $K^+ > 3.3\text{ mEq/L}$.
   * If $K^+$ is between $3.3\text{ and }5.2\text{ mEq/L}$: Add $20\text{ to }30\text{ mEq KCl}$ per liter of IV fluid; initiate insulin infusion.
   * If $K^+ > 5.2\text{ mEq/L}$: Do not give potassium; start insulin and re-check serum potassium every 2 hours.
3. **Intravenous Regular Insulin:** Administer regular insulin continuous infusion at $0.1\text{ units/kg/hr}$ (or $0.14\text{ units/kg/hr}$ without bolus). Aim for a gradual glucose reduction of $50\text{ to }75\text{ mg/dL/hr}$.
4. **Resolution Criteria:** DKA is resolved when blood glucose is $< 200\text{ mg/dL}$ along with at least two of: serum bicarbonate $\ge 15\text{ mEq/L}$, venous pH $> 7.30$, and calculated anion gap $\le 12\text{ mEq/L}$. Transition to subcutaneous basal insulin 2 hours prior to stopping IV insulin infusion to prevent rebound ketoacidosis.
