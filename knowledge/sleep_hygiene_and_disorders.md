# Healthy Sleep Habits and Insomnia Overview

## Introduction to Healthy Sleep
Sleep is a vital biological function that allows the body and brain to recover, consolidate memory, and maintain hormonal and cardiovascular health. Most healthy adults require between 7 to 9 hours of quality, restful sleep per night. Children and adolescents require more sleep, ranging from 8 to 14 hours depending on age.

## Principles of Sleep Hygiene
Good sleep hygiene consists of behavioral and environmental practices that promote consistent, uninterrupted sleep:
- **Consistent Schedule:** Go to bed and wake up at the exact same time every day, including on weekends and holidays. This synchronizes your circadian biological clock.
- **Sleep Environment:** Keep the bedroom quiet, dark, and cool (typically around 65°F to 68°F or 18°C to 20°C). Use blackout curtains or an eye mask if ambient light is unavoidable.
- **Screen and Blue Light Restrictions:** Turn off smartphones, computers, tablets, and televisions at least 60 minutes before bedtime. Electronic screens emit short-wavelength blue light that suppresses pineal melatonin production, delaying sleep onset.
- **Caffeine and Stimulant Management:** Avoid consuming caffeinated beverages (coffee, energy drinks, black or green tea, colas) at least 6 hours before bedtime.
- **Alcohol and Meals:** Avoid heavy, spicy, or high-sugar meals within 3 hours of sleep, which can cause indigestion and sleep disruption. While alcohol is a sedative that might accelerate falling asleep, it severely impairs REM sleep architecture and causes frequent nighttime awakenings.
- **Regular Daytime Physical Activity:** Engaging in moderate physical exercise earlier in the day improves sleep depth, but vigorous cardiovascular workouts should be completed at least 2 to 3 hours before retiring.

## Common Sleep Disorders
- **Insomnia:** Difficulty falling asleep, staying asleep, or waking up too early feeling unrefreshed. Acute insomnia is often triggered by transient stress or schedule disruptions, while chronic insomnia persists for 3 months or longer.
- **Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA):** A condition characterized by repeated partial or complete airway collapse during sleep, resulting in pauses in breathing, loud snoring, gasping, and severe daytime exhaustion.
- **Restless Legs Syndrome (RLS):** An uncontrollable urge to move the legs, particularly during evening hours or periods of inactivity.

## When to Seek Medical Evaluation
Consult a physician or sleep specialist if:
- Sleep difficulties persist for longer than 4 weeks and interfere with daytime productivity or mood.
- You or your bed partner observe gasping, choking, or breathing cessation during sleep.
- You experience severe excessive daytime sleepiness, such as falling asleep while driving or talking.
