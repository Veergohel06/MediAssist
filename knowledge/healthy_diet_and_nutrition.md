# Healthy Diet and Nutrition Facts

## WHO Dietary Guidelines for Adults
A healthy diet protects against malnutrition in all its forms, as well as noncommunicable diseases (NCDs) such as diabetes, heart disease, stroke, and cancer. The World Health Organization establishes the following evidence-based nutritional principles:

### Core Food Groups
- **Fruit, vegetables, legumes, nuts, and whole grains:**
  - Consume at least 400 grams (approximately 5 portions) of fruits and vegetables daily, excluding starchy roots such as potatoes or cassava.
  - Choose unprocessed whole grains (e.g., oats, whole wheat, brown rice, quinoa, millet) rather than refined grains.

### Fats and Lipids
- Total fat intake should not exceed 30% of total energy intake to prevent unhealthy weight gain.
- Prefer unsaturated fats (found in fish, avocados, nuts, and sunflower, soybean, canola, and olive oils) over saturated fats (found in fatty meat, butter, palm and coconut oil, cream, cheese, and lard).
- Saturated fats should represent less than 10% of total caloric intake.
- Industrial trans-fats (found in fried foods, commercial baked goods, and pre-packaged frozen snacks) should be eliminated completely (less than 1% of total energy).

### Free Sugars
- Free sugar intake should be reduced to less than 10% of total daily energy intake (equivalent to roughly 50 grams or about 12 level teaspoons for a 2,000-calorie diet).
- Further reduction to below 5% of total energy provides additional proven health benefits, specifically preventing dental caries and metabolic disorders.
- Free sugars include all monosaccharides and disaccharides added to foods and drinks by manufacturers or cooks, plus sugars naturally present in honey, syrups, fruit juices, and fruit juice concentrates.

### Salt, Sodium, and Potassium
- Limit salt consumption to less than 5 grams per day (approximately 1 level teaspoon), which corresponds to under 2,000 mg of elemental sodium.
- Ensure all dietary salt is iodized to support normal thyroid hormone synthesis.
- Increase dietary potassium intake (at least 3,510 mg per day) by eating dark leafy greens, pulses, nuts, and fresh fruits to help mitigate blood pressure elevations.
