# Tension Headaches and Migraines: Causes, Symptoms, and Management

## Understanding Tension Headaches
Tension headaches (also termed tension-type headaches or stress headaches) are the most common primary headache disorder. They are characterized by a dull, aching, non-pulsatile pain that affects both sides of the head. Patients frequently describe the sensation as a tight band or vice constricting the forehead, temples, or back of the neck and scalp.

## Common Triggers and Contributing Factors
- **Psychological and Emotional Stress:** Chronic daily stressors, anxiety, emotional strain, and depression frequently provoke muscular contractions in the head and neck muscles.
- **Physical Muscle Strain & Posture:** Prolonged poor ergonomics, such as slouching at a desk, looking down at mobile phones for hours ("tech neck"), or holding a phone between the shoulder and ear.
- **Ocular Strain:** Uncorrected vision impairments or prolonged glare and screen exposure without visual breaks (the 20-20-20 rule helps: look 20 feet away every 20 minutes for 20 seconds).
- **Physical Deprivation:** Dehydration, missed or irregular meals causing hypoglycemia, and sleep deprivation.
- **Caffeine Fluctuations:** Sudden cessation of habitual caffeine intake (caffeine withdrawal).

## Difference Between Tension Headaches and Migraines
- **Tension Headache:** Typically bilateral, mild-to-moderate dull ache, not aggravated by routine physical activity, and usually absent of severe nausea, vomiting, or visual auras.
- **Migraine:** Typically unilateral (one-sided), moderate-to-severe throbbing or pulsating pain, worsens with normal physical activity (walking, stairs), and often accompanied by sensitivity to light (photophobia), sound (phonophobia), nausea, and occasionally sensory/visual auras.

## Non-Pharmacological Home Remedies & Preventive Measures
- **Heat or Cold Therapy:** Applying a warm heating pad or warm towel to the neck and shoulders eases tight muscles; a cool gel pack to the forehead can relieve dull aching.
- **Ergonomic Adjustments:** Adjusting chair height, monitor distance (eye level), and using an ergonomic keyboard and mouse.
- **Hydration & Regular Meals:** Consuming water steadily throughout the day and eating balanced meals to stabilize blood glucose.
- **Stress Reduction Techniques:** Progressive muscle relaxation (PMR), guided diaphragmatic breathing, meditation, and gentle cervical spine stretches.

## Red Flag Symptoms (Seek Emergency Care)
Seek immediate emergency medical attention if a headache is:
- Sudden and explosively severe, reaching peak intensity within seconds ("thunderclap headache").
- Accompanied by high fever, stiff neck, confusion, seizure, double vision, weakness, numbness, or difficulty speaking.
- New onset following a head injury, fall, or blunt trauma.
- Progressively worsening over days or weeks in individuals over 50 years old.
