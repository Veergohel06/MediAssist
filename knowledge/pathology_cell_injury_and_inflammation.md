# Pathology: Cellular Injury, Cell Death Patterns, and Inflammatory Cascade

## Reversible versus Irreversible Cell Injury

Cellular injury occurs when environmental stresses exceed the adaptive capacity of a cell. The sequence progresses from cellular adaptation to reversible cell injury, and finally to irreversible cell injury and cell death.

### 1. Hallmarks of Reversible Cell Injury
- **ATP Depletion & Sodium-Potassium Pump Failure:** Reduced oxidative phosphorylation diminishes ATP, leading to failure of the plasma membrane Na+/K+ ATPase pump. Intracellular Na+ and water accumulate, producing acute cellular swelling (hydropic change or vacuolar degeneration).
- **Mitochondrial Swelling:** Loss of membrane potential results in reversible mitochondrial condensation and swelling.
- **Detachment of Ribosomes:** Endoplasmic reticulum dilates and ribosomes detach, reducing total cellular protein synthesis.
- **Plasma Membrane Blebbing:** Cytoskeletal dysfunction causes surface blebs, but the plasma membrane remains structurally intact.
- **Fatty Change (Steatosis):** Observed predominantly in hepatocytes and myocardium due to impaired lipid metabolism and lipoprotein export.

### 2. Transition Point to Irreversible Cell Injury
Cell injury becomes irreversible when two critical thresholds are crossed:
1. **Inability to Reverse Mitochondrial Dysfunction:** Persistent absence of ATP generation and permanent opening of the mitochondrial permeability transition pore (MPTP).
2. **Profound Disturbances in Membrane Function:** Irreversible breakdown of plasma membranes, lysosomal membranes, and mitochondrial membranes. Massive intracellular influx of extracellular calcium activates hydrolytic enzymes including phospholipases, proteases, and endonucleases, causing enzymatic digestion of the cell.

## Patterns of Tissue Necrosis

Necrosis is the morphological expression of accidental, unregulated cell death characterized by cell swelling (oncosis), membrane denaturation, and inflammatory host response.

| Necrosis Pattern | Predominant Mechanism | Morphological & Histological Features | Classic Clinical Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Coagulative Necrosis** | Protein denaturation with preservation of structural tissue framework for days | Ghost cells / tombstone outlines without nuclei; eosinophilic cytoplasm | Myocardial infarction, renal cortical infarction (all solid organ infarcts except brain) |
| **Liquefactive Necrosis** | Enzymatic digestion by lysosomal enzymes and neutrophil hydrolases | Complete dissolution of dead tissue into a viscous, liquid mass; cystic cavities | Ischemic cerebral infarction (stroke), bacterial & fungal abscesses |
| **Caseous Necrosis** | Combined protein denaturation and lipid debris breakdown in granulomas | Friable, yellow-white "cheese-like" gross appearance; amorphous granular debris surrounded by epithelioid histiocytes and Langhans giant cells | Mycobacterium tuberculosis infection, systemic fungal infections (Histoplasmosis) |
| **Fat Necrosis** | Enzymatic (lipase-mediated) or traumatic saponification of triglycerides | Chalky-white deposits; shadowy outlines of necrotic adipocytes with basophilic calcium soaps | Acute pancreatitis (enzymatic), trauma to adipose tissue in female breast |
| **Fibrinoid Necrosis** | Immune-complex deposition combined with fibrin leakage in vessel walls | Amorphous, brightly eosinophilic, smudgy appearance in arterial walls | Polyarteritis nodosa, malignant hypertension, hyperacute transplant rejection |
| **Gangrenous Necrosis** | Clinical term: Coagulative necrosis modified by liquefactive bacterial superinfection | Dry gangrene (predominantly coagulative, ischemic mummification) vs Wet gangrene (liquefactive bacterial superinfection) | Diabetic foot gangrene, mesenteric vascular ischemia |

## Molecular Mechanisms of Apoptosis

Apoptosis is programmed, ATP-dependent cell death characterized by cell shrinkage, chromatin condensation, apoptotic body formation, and phagocytosis without eliciting an inflammatory response.

### 1. Intrinsic (Mitochondrial) Pathway
- **Initiating Triggers:** DNA damage (via p53), growth factor deprivation, cellular stress, or misfolded protein accumulation in the ER.
- **Bcl-2 Family Sensor Regulation:** Pro-apoptotic BH3-only proteins (Bim, Bid, Bad, Puma) are activated and neutralize anti-apoptotic guardians (Bcl-2, Bcl-xL, Mcl-1).
- **MOMP Permeabilization:** Pro-apoptotic effectors **Bax** and **Bak** oligomerize in the outer mitochondrial membrane, forming pores that release **Cytochrome c** into the cytosol.
- **Apoptosome Assembly:** Cytosolic Cytochrome c binds to **Apaf-1** (Apoptotic Protease Activating Factor-1) and dATP, forming the wheel-like wheel apoptosome heptamer.
- **Initiator Caspase Activation:** The apoptosome cleaves and activates **Procaspase-9** into active **Caspase-9**.

### 2. Extrinsic (Death Receptor) Pathway
- **Death Receptors:** Cell-surface receptors belonging to the Tumor Necrosis Factor Receptor (TNFR) superfamily containing cytoplasmic Death Domains (DD), including **Fas (CD95)** and **TNFR1**.
- **Ligand Binding:** Fas Ligand (FasL on cytotoxic T cells) binds Fas, causing trimerization of the receptor and recruitment of the adaptor protein **FADD** (Fas-Associated Death Domain).
- **DISC Complex:** FADD recruits **Procaspase-8** (or Procaspase-10), forming the Death-Inducing Signaling Complex (DISC). Caspase-8 is activated via self-cleavage.
- **Crosstalk to Intrinsic Pathway:** Caspase-8 can cleave the BH3-only protein **Bid** into truncated **tBid**, which translocates to mitochondria to activate Bax/Bak.

### 3. Execution Phase
- Both initiator Caspase-9 (intrinsic) and Caspase-8 (extrinsic) converge to cleave executioner caspases: **Caspase-3**, **Caspase-6**, and **Caspase-7**.
- Executioner caspases cleave cytoskeletal proteins, nuclear scaffold lamins, and activate CAD (Caspase-Activated DNase) by cleaving its inhibitor ICAD, producing internucleosomal DNA fragmentation in 180–200 base-pair increments (DNA laddering).
- Phosphatidylserine flips from the inner leaflet to the outer leaflet of the plasma membrane, serving as an "eat-me" signal for macrophage phagocytosis without cytokine release.

## Acute versus Chronic Inflammation Mechanisms

### 1. Vascular Changes in Acute Inflammation
- **Transient Vasoconstriction:** Lasts seconds, mediated by neurogenic reflex and endothelin.
- **Arteriolar Vasodilation:** Mediated by nitric oxide (NO) and histamine, resulting in increased blood flow (rubor and calor).
- **Increased Vascular Permeability:** Endothelial cell contraction mediated by histamine, bradykinin, and leukotrienes (C4, D4, E4) produces intercellular gaps in postcapillary venules, leading to protein-rich fluid exudation (tumor) and hemoconcentration with stasis.

### 2. Leukocyte Extravasation Cascade
1. **Margination & Rolling:** Leukocytes push to the vessel periphery. Rolling is mediated by low-affinity interactions between leukocyte sialyl-Lewis X and endothelial **selectins** (P-selectin mobilized from Weibel-Palade bodies; E-selectin induced by TNF and IL-1).
2. **Firm Adhesion:** Chemoattractants activate leukocyte **integrins** (LFA-1 and Mac-1) to high-affinity conformation, binding to endothelial ligands **ICAM-1** and **VCAM-1**.
3. **Transmigration (Diapedesis):** Leukocytes traverse the endothelial wall primarily at junctions, mediated by **PECAM-1 (CD31)**.
4. **Chemotaxis:** Leukocytes migrate toward the injury site along chemical gradients (C5a, LTB4, bacterial N-formyl peptides, IL-8).

### 3. Mediators of Inflammation Summary
- **Vasodilation:** Histamine, Prostaglandin I2 (PGI2/prostacyclin), PGD2, PGE2, Nitric Oxide.
- **Increased Permeability:** Histamine, Serotonin, Bradykinin, Leukotrienes C4/D4/E4, C3a, C5a.
- **Chemotaxis & Leukocyte Activation:** C5a, Leukotriene B4 (LTB4), Interleukin-8 (CXCL8).
- **Fever:** Interleukin-1 (IL-1), Tumor Necrosis Factor-alpha (TNF-alpha), Interleukin-6 (IL-6), which stimulate hypothalamic PGE2 synthesis.
- **Pain:** Bradykinin, Prostaglandin E2 (sensitizes nociceptive afferents).
- **Tissue Damage:** Neutrophil lysosomal enzymes, Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS).
