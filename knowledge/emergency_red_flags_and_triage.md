# Medical Emergencies and Warning Signs Guide

## When to Call Emergency Medical Services Immediately
A medical emergency is an acute injury or illness that poses an immediate risk to a person's life or long-term health. When red flag warning signs appear, immediate activation of emergency medical dispatch (such as **112** or **108** in India, **911** in the US, or **999** in the UK) is imperative.

## Critical Red Flag Warning Signs
Medical professionals recognize several key symptom constellations that necessitate immediate hospital emergency department evaluation:

### 1. Chest Pain and Cardiac Warning Signs
- Crushing, heavy pressure, squeezing, or fullness in the center of the chest.
- Pain radiating to the jaw, neck, back, epigastrium, or down the left or both arms.
- Chest discomfort accompanied by unexplained shortness of breath, profuse cold sweating (diaphoresis), dizziness, lightheadedness, or sudden nausea.
- *Clinical rule:* Never drive yourself or wait to see if chest pain dissipates.

### 2. Acute Stroke Warning Signs (The FAST Protocol)
- **F (Face Drooping):** One side of the face droops or is numb. An uneven smile indicates cranial nerve involvement.
- **A (Arm Weakness):** Sudden weakness or numbness in one arm or leg, or inability to raise both arms evenly.
- **S (Speech Difficulty):** Slurred speech, inability to repeat simple phrases, or confusion in comprehending spoken language.
- **T (Time to Call Emergency Services):** Immediate medical intervention within the first 3 to 4.5 hours is critical for thrombolytic or endovascular therapy.

### 3. Severe Respiratory Distress
- Severe shortness of breath or sensation of suffocation.
- Inability to speak in full sentences without gasping for breath.
- Blue, gray, or pale discoloration of lips, tongue, or nail beds (cyanosis indicating systemic hypoxia).
- Stridor (high-pitched whistling sound during inhalation indicating upper airway obstruction).

### 4. Sudden Severe Neurological Symptoms
- A sudden, blinding, explosive headache reaching maximum intensity within seconds (termed a "thunderclap headache"), which may signal subarachnoid hemorrhage.
- Severe headache accompanied by a high fever, stiff neck, photophobia, confusion, or a purpuric rash (signs of acute bacterial meningitis).
- Sudden onset of seizures in an individual without prior seizure disorder.

### 5. Anaphylaxis (Severe Systemic Allergic Reaction)
- Sudden swelling of the tongue, lips, throat, or uvula.
- Feeling of the throat constricting or severe difficulty swallowing and breathing.
- Generalized hives, rapid pulse, dizziness, or collapse following an insect sting, food intake, or medication.

## Routine vs. Urgent Medical Consultation
- **Routine Consultation:** Mild symptoms of gradual onset (e.g., mild nasal congestion, occasional joint stiffness, minor fatigue) can be addressed via an outpatient scheduled appointment.
- **Urgent Care / Emergency Department:** Sudden onset, severe pain, progressive worsening, bleeding, high fever unresponsive to antipyretics, or signs of organ compromise warrant immediate clinical evaluation.
