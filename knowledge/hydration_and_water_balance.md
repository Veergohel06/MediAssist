# Water and Daily Hydration Requirements

## Why Water is Essential
Water accounts for approximately 55% to 60% of an adult human body weight. It plays essential roles in every organ system:
- Regulating core body temperature through sweating and respiration.
- Lubricating and cushioning joints and spinal discs.
- Transporting dissolved nutrients, oxygen, and hormones to cellular tissues.
- Assisting renal clearance of waste metabolites and nitrogenous compounds.
- Preventing constipation and facilitating gastrointestinal motility.

## Recommended Fluid Intake
While individual water needs fluctuate depending on climate, temperature, physical exertion, and overall health status, general health guidelines suggest:
- **Women:** Approximately 2.7 liters (about 11 cups) of total fluid per day from all beverages and moisture-rich foods.
- **Men:** Approximately 3.7 liters (about 15.5 cups) of total fluid per day from all beverages and moisture-rich foods.
- Roughly 20% of daily water intake typically comes from solid foods (especially fruits and vegetables such as cucumbers, watermelons, tomatoes, and oranges), with the remaining 80% provided by drinking water and non-sugary beverages.

## Recognizing Signs of Dehydration
- **Early / Mild Dehydration:** Thirst, dark amber or cloudy urine, reduced urination frequency, dry mouth, mild lightheadedness, fatigue, and tension headaches.
- **Severe Dehydration (Medical Emergency):** Extreme thirst, sunken eyes, rapid heart rate (tachycardia), low blood pressure (hypotension), confusion or lethargy, lack of sweating despite heat, and minimal or no urine output for over 8 hours.

## Practical Strategies for Consistent Hydration
- Keep a reusable water bottle visible on your desk or when traveling.
- Drink a glass of water upon waking to replenish fluids lost through nighttime respiration.
- Infuse water with natural flavorings such as lemon slices, cucumber, mint leaves, or berries if plain water tastes bland.
- Drink water before, during, and after strenuous physical exertion or time spent in hot environments.
- Monitor your urine color: pale straw or light yellow signifies optimal hydration, while dark yellow indicates a need for fluids.
- Caution for special conditions: Individuals with congestive heart failure, chronic kidney disease (CKD), or severe liver cirrhosis should adhere strictly to their physician's individualized fluid limits.
