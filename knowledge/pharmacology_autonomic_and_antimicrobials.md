# Pharmacology: Autonomic Neurotransmission and Core Antimicrobial Classes

## Autonomic Nervous System Receptors and Second Messenger Cascades

The autonomic nervous system regulates involuntary visceral functions through sympathetic (adrenergic) and parasympathetic (cholinergic) divisions.

### 1. Adrenergic Receptor Subtypes and Signal Transduction
- **Alpha-1 ($\alpha_1$) Receptors:** Coupled to $G_q$ protein. Activates phospholipase C (PLC) $\rightarrow$ generates inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate ($IP_3$) and diacylglycerol (DAG) $\rightarrow$ increases intracellular $Ca^{2+}$.
  * *Actions:* Vascular smooth muscle contraction (vasoconstriction, increased systemic vascular resistance), pupillary dilator muscle contraction (mydriasis), urinary sphincter contraction.
  * *Agonists:* Phenylephrine, Methoxamine.
  * *Antagonists:* Prazosin, Doxazosin, Tamsulosin ($\alpha_{1A}$ selective for benign prostatic hyperplasia).
- **Alpha-2 ($\alpha_2$) Receptors:** Coupled to $G_i$ protein. Inhibits adenylyl cyclase $\rightarrow$ decreases intracellular cAMP.
  * *Actions:* Presynaptic autoreceptor inhibition (decreases norepinephrine release), decreased sympathetic outflow in CNS, decreased insulin release.
  * *Agonists:* Clonidine, Methyldopa, Dexmedetomidine.
  * *Antagonists:* Yohimbine.
- **Beta-1 ($\beta_1$) Receptors:** Coupled to $G_s$ protein. Stimulates adenylyl cyclase $\rightarrow$ increases cAMP $\rightarrow$ activates Protein Kinase A (PKA).
  * *Actions:* Located primarily in heart and juxtaglomerular cells. Positive inotropic (contractility), chronotropic (heart rate), dromotropic (conduction velocity) effects; stimulates renin release.
  * *Agonists:* Dobutamine, Isoproterenol.
  * *Antagonists (Cardioselective $\beta_1$ Blockers):* Metoprolol, Atenolol, Esmolol, Bisoprolol.
- **Beta-2 ($\beta_2$) Receptors:** Coupled to $G_s$ protein. Increases cAMP $\rightarrow$ PKA $\rightarrow$ phosphorylates myosin light chain kinase (inactivating it) $\rightarrow$ smooth muscle relaxation.
  * *Actions:* Bronchodilation, vasodilation in skeletal muscle vascular beds, uterine relaxation (tocolysis), stimulation of hepatic glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis, hypokalemia via cellular $K^+$ uptake.
  * *Agonists:* Salbutamol (Albuterol), Salmeterol, Formoterol, Terbutaline.
  * *Antagonists (Non-selective $\beta$ Blockers):* Propranolol, Timolol, Nadolol.
- **Beta-3 ($\beta_3$) Receptors:** Coupled to $G_s$. Located in adipose tissue (lipolysis) and detrusor muscle of bladder (relaxation). Agonist: Mirabegron (used in overactive bladder).

### 2. Cholinergic Muscarinic Receptors
- **$M_1, M_3, M_5$ Receptors:** Coupled to $G_q$ ($IP_3$/DAG cascade, increased intracellular $Ca^{2+}$).
  * $M_1$: CNS and gastric parietal cells (stimulates gastric acid secretion).
  * $M_3$: Exocrine glands (lacrimation, salivation, sweating), visceral smooth muscle (bronchoconstriction, GI hypermotility, detrusor contraction/urination), vascular endothelium (stimulates NO release via eNOS producing vasodilation), ciliary muscle contraction (accommodation for near vision and pupillary sphincter contraction $\rightarrow$ miosis).
- **$M_2, M_4$ Receptors:** Coupled to $G_i$ (inhibits adenylyl cyclase, opens inwardly rectifying $K^+$ channels).
  * $M_2$: Located in cardiac nodal tissue (SA and AV nodes). Slows heart rate (negative chronotropy) and decreases AV conduction velocity (negative dromotropy).

### 3. Organophosphate Toxicity and Management
Organophosphates (e.g., malathion, parathion, echothiophate, sarin) irreversibly bind and phosphorylate the esteratic site of acetylcholinesterase (AChE), causing massive accumulation of acetylcholine at muscarinic and nicotinic synapses.
- **Muscarinic Hyperstimulation (SLUDGE-BAM / DUMBELS):** Salivation, Lacrimation, Urination, Defecation, Gastrointestinal cramps, Emesis, Bronchospasm, Bronchorrhea, Bradycardia, Miosis.
- **Nicotinic Manifestations:** Muscle fasciculations, weakness, diaphragmatic paralysis, autonomic instability.
- **Antidotal Treatment:**
  1. **Atropine (Competitive Muscarinic Antagonist):** Administered intravenously in escalating doses every 5–10 minutes until full atropinization is achieved. *Endpoint:* Clearing of bronchorrhea and dry pulmonary secretions (not pupillary dilation or heart rate).
  2. **Pralidoxime (2-PAM, AChE Reactivator):** Cleaves the phosphate group from the acetylcholinesterase active site before irreversible chemical covalent bond strengthening ("aging") occurs. Reverses nicotinic neuromuscular weakness.

## Core Systemic Antimicrobial Classes

| Drug Class | Molecular Target & Mechanism | Spectrum of Activity | Clinical Indications | Key Toxicities & High-Yield Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Beta-Lactams (Penicillins, Cephalosporins, Carbapenems)** | Bind to Penicillin-Binding Proteins (PBPs / transpeptidases); inhibit peptidoglycan cross-linking, activating autolysins | Gram-positive cocci, Gram-negative rods (broadened with beta-lactamase inhibitors: clavulanate, tazobactam) | Pneumonia, meningitis, cellulitis, sepsis, endocarditis | IgE-mediated anaphylaxis, maculopapular rash, interstitial nephritis, seizures (high-dose imipenem/penicillin) |
| **Aminoglycosides (Gentamicin, Amikacin, Tobramycin)** | Irreversibly bind the 30S ribosomal subunit; cause mRNA misreading and block translation initiation | Aerobic Gram-negative bacilli (Pseudomonas, Enterobacteriaceae); synergistic with beta-lactams for enterococci | Hospital-acquired pneumonia, complicated bacteremia, endocarditis synergy | Dose-dependent Ototoxicity (vestibular and cochlear hair cell damage, irreversible); Nephrotoxicity (acute tubular necrosis); Neuromuscular blockade |
| **Tetracyclines (Doxycycline, Minocycline)** | Reversibly bind to the 30S ribosomal subunit; prevent access of aminoacyl-tRNA to the ribosomal acceptor (A) site | Broad spectrum: atypical pathogens, rickettsiae, spirochetes, intracellular bacteria | Lyme disease, Rocky Mountain spotted fever, Chlamydia trachomatis, Mycoplasma pneumonia | Tooth discoloration and enamel hypoplasia in children under 8; photosensitivity; esophagitis; contraindicated in pregnancy |
| **Macrolides (Azithromycin, Clarithromycin, Erythromycin)** | Reversibly bind to 50S ribosomal subunit (23S rRNA); inhibit transpeptidation and translocation of peptidyl-tRNA | Atypical pneumonia pathogens (Legionella, Mycoplasma, Chlamydophila), Bordetella pertussis, H. pylori | Community-acquired pneumonia, pertussis, chlamydia, non-gonococcal urethritis | QT interval prolongation (torsades de pointes risk), CYP3A4 inhibition (clarithromycin), motilin receptor stimulation (gastrointestinal hypermotility) |
| **Fluoroquinolones (Ciprofloxacin, Levofloxacin, Moxifloxacin)** | Inhibit bacterial DNA Gyrase (Topoisomerase II) and Topoisomerase IV; block DNA replication and transcription | Broad Gram-negative (Ciprofloxacin) and respiratory Gram-positive/atypical (Levofloxacin, Moxifloxacin) | Complicated UTIs, pyelonephritis, infectious diarrhea, respiratory infections | Tendinitis and Achilles tendon rupture, QT prolongation, cartilage damage in growing joints, CNS excitation (seizures) |
| **Antifolates (Trimethoprim-Sulfamethoxazole / TMP-SMX)** | Sequential blockade: Sulfamethoxazole inhibits Dihydropteroate Synthase (DHPS); Trimethoprim inhibits Dihydrofolate Reductase (DHFR) | Broad Gram-positive and Gram-negative; Pneumocystis jirovecii, Nocardia, Toxoplasma gondii | Pneumocystis pneumonia (PJP prophylaxis & treatment), uncomplicated UTI, CA-MRSA skin infections | Steven-Johnson Syndrome (SJS/TEN), megaloblastic anemia (treat with folinic acid/leucovorin), hyperkalemia, kernicterus in neonates |
