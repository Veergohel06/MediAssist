# Microbiology: Diagnostic Bacteriology, Mycobacterium Tuberculosis, and Parasitology

## Bacterial Cell Wall Architecture and Gram Staining Principles

The fundamental taxonomic and diagnostic differentiation of eubacteria is based on cell envelope architecture.

| Structural Feature | Gram-Positive Bacteria | Gram-Negative Bacteria |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Peptidoglycan Layer** | Thick multilayered meshwork (20–80 nm), highly cross-linked | Thin single- or double-layered meshwork (1–7 nm) |
| **Teichoic & Lipoteichoic Acids** | Present (essential surface antigens and adhesins; induce host IL-1 and TNF) | Absent |
| **Outer Membrane** | Absent | Present (asymmetric lipid bilayer: inner phospholipid, outer Lipopolysaccharide) |
| **Lipopolysaccharide (LPS / Endotoxin)** | Absent | Present (Lipid A: toxic moiety inducing septic shock; Core polysaccharide; O-antigen repeat units) |
| **Periplasmic Space** | Narrow or absent | Prominent gel-filled space between inner and outer membranes (contains beta-lactamases, transport proteins) |
| **Porin Channels** | Absent | Present in outer membrane (regulates passive diffusion of hydrophilic molecules and antibiotics) |
| **Gram Stain Color Reaction** | Retains Crystal Violet-Iodine complex; appears **Dark Purple/Blue** | De-colorized by alcohol/acetone; takes up counterstain Safranin; appears **Pink/Red** |

## Pathogenesis and Diagnosis of Mycobacterium Tuberculosis

Mycobacterium tuberculosis (MTB) is an obligate aerobe, slow-growing bacillus with a unique, lipid-rich cell wall containing **mycolic acids**, arabinogalactan, and cord factor (trehalose dimycolate, which inhibits leukocyte migration and disrupts mitochondrial respiration).

### 1. Primary Infection and Granuloma Formation
- **Inhalation & Alveolar Deposition:** Droplet nuclei (1–5 $\mu m$) deposit in lower part of upper lobe or upper part of lower lobe (subpleural region).
- **Macrophage Uptake:** Phagocytosed by alveolar macrophages via mannose and complement receptors. MTB inhibits phagosome-lysosome fusion via early endosomal autoantigen-1 (EEA1) exclusion and coronin-1 retention.
- **Cell-Mediated Immunity (Type IV Hypersensitivity):** After 3 weeks, dendritic cells present mycobacterial antigens (ESAT-6, CFP-10) to CD4+ T helper cells in hilar lymph nodes. Macrophages produce IL-12, driving **Th1 differentiation**. Th1 cells secrete **Interferon-gamma (IFN-$\gamma$)**, which activates macrophages to synthesize nitric oxide synthase (iNOS), reactive nitrogen species, and TNF-$\alpha$.
- **Ghon Focus & Complex:** Activated macrophages transform into **epithelioid histiocytes** and fuse to form multinucleated **Langhans giant cells**. The subpleural granuloma focus plus draining hilar lymphadenopathy forms the **Ghon Complex**. When calcified on chest radiography, it is designated the **Ranke Complex**.
- **Caseation:** Central caseous necrosis creates an anoxic, acidic microenvironment restricting bacillary replication into a dormant, latent state.

### 2. Secondary (Reactivation) Tuberculosis
- Occurs during host immunosuppression (HIV/AIDS, TNF inhibitor therapy like infliximab, malnutrition, diabetes, corticosteroids).
- Classically involves the **apical and posterior segments of the upper lobes** where high ventilation-perfusion ratios ($V/Q$) provide abundant oxygen.
- Cavitary lesions develop with erosion into bronchial airways, releasing vast numbers of bacilli into sputum (highly contagious; presents with hemoptysis, chronic cough, night sweats, cachexia).

### 3. Laboratory Diagnosis
- **Microscopy:** Sputum smear stained with **Ziehl-Neelsen (ZN)** acid-fast stain (hot method) or **Kinyoun** (cold method). Bacilli resist acid-alcohol de-colorization and appear as bright red/pink slender, beaded rods on a methylene blue background. **Auramine-O fluorescent stain** provides higher diagnostic sensitivity for screening.
- **Molecular Testing (GeneXpert MTB/RIF):** Real-time automated nested PCR assay detecting MTB DNA and mutations in the *rpoB* gene conferring rifampicin resistance within 2 hours.
- **Culture:** Gold standard for confirmation and drug susceptibility.
  * Lowenstein-Jensen (LJ) solid egg-based medium: Rough, buff, tough colonies visible after 3 to 6 weeks.
  * Middlebrook 7H9/7H10 or Automated liquid broth (MGIT - Mycobacterial Growth Indicator Tube): Detection within 1 to 2 weeks.

## Enteric Bacterial Pathogens: Salmonella versus Shigella

Enteric Gram-negative bacilli of the family *Enterobacteriaceae* are major causes of febrile illness and diarrheal syndromes.

### 1. Salmonella enterica serovar Typhi (Enteric / Typhoid Fever)
- **Pathogenesis:** Ingested via contaminated water/food. Passes through acid gastric barrier, invades ileal Peyer's patches via microfold (**M cells**), and survives inside unactivated macrophages using Type III Secretion Systems (T3SS on SPI-1 and SPI-2).
- **Systemic Dissemination:** Carried via mesenteric lymph nodes into thoracic duct and enters bloodstream (transient primary bacteremia), then seeds the reticuloendothelial system (liver, spleen, bone marrow, gallbladder).
- **Clinical Course:** Step-ladder fever pattern, relative bradycardia (Faget sign), splenomegaly, transient faint salmon-pink blanching maculopapular rash on trunk (**Rose spots**), and pea-soup diarrhea or constipation. In week 3, ulceration of Peyer's patches along the longitudinal axis of bowel may cause severe intestinal hemorrhage or perforation.
- **Diagnostic Timeline (BASU Mnemonic):**
  * Week 1: **B**lood culture positive in 80–90% of cases.
  * Week 2: **A**ntibody detection by Widal agglutination test (TO $\ge$ 1:160, TH $\ge$ 1:160).
  * Week 3: **S**tool culture positive.
  * Week 4: **U**rine culture positive.

### 2. Shigella dysenteriae (Bacillary Dysentery)
- **Extremely Low Infectious Dose:** As few as 10–100 organisms cause clinical disease. Resistant to gastric acid.
- **Colonic Epithelial Invasion:** Invades colonic enterocytes basolaterally via M cells; polymerizes host actin tails (IcsA) to rocket into adjacent epithelial cells, avoiding extracellular immune detection.
- **Toxin Production:** *Shigella dysenteriae* Type 1 produces **Shiga toxin** (AB5 toxin). Subunit B binds host cell receptor Gb3 (globotriaosylceramide); Subunit A cleaves 28S rRNA of the 60S ribosomal subunit, halting host protein synthesis.
- **Clinical Picture:** High fever, severe abdominal cramps, tenesmus, and frequent small-volume bloody, mucoid stools (dysentery).
- **Complications:** **Hemolytic Uremic Syndrome (HUS)**: Microangiopathic hemolytic anemia, thrombocytopenia, and acute renal failure triggered by endothelial damage in glomerular capillaries.

## Medical Parasitology: Plasmodium Life Cycle and Malaria

Human malaria is caused by five protozoan species of the genus *Plasmodium* (*P. falciparum*, *P. vivax*, *P. malariae*, *P. ovale*, *P. knowlesi*), transmitted by the bite of infected female *Anopheles* mosquitoes.

### 1. Life Cycle Steps
1. **Exo-Erythrocytic (Hepatic) Phase:** Mosquito injects **sporozoites** during blood meal $\rightarrow$ travel through bloodstream to hepatocytes within 30 minutes $\rightarrow$ asexual replication (**schizogony**) produces tens of thousands of **merozoites**.
   * *Hypnozoite Stage:* In **Plasmodium vivax** and **Plasmodium ovale**, a fraction of sporozoites differentiate into dormant **hypnozoites** in liver cells, causing clinical relapses months to years later unless cleared with **Primaquine** or **Tafenoquine** (after screening for G6PD deficiency).
2. **Erythrocytic Phase:** Merozoites invade erythrocytes. Sequential morphology: Ring form (early trophozoite) $\rightarrow$ Ameboid/compact trophozoite $\rightarrow$ Schizont containing daughter merozoites $\rightarrow$ RBC lysis releasing merozoites, pyrogenic hemozoin pigment, and GPI-anchors that stimulate TNF-$\alpha$, producing paroxysms of shaking chills and high fever.
   * Fever Paroxysm Cadence: *P. vivax* and *P. ovale* = Benign tertian (48-hour cycle); *P. malariae* = Quartan (72-hour cycle); *P. falciparum* = Malignant tertian (irregular 36–48-hour cycle).
3. **Sexual Phase (Sporogonic Cycle):** A subset of intra-erythrocytic parasites differentiate into female (macrogametocyte) and male (microgametocyte) forms, which are taken up by mosquitoes during feeding. Fertilization in mosquito midgut produces an **ookinete**, which penetrates the midgut wall to form an **oocyst**, ultimately bursting to release infectious sporozoites into salivary glands.

### 2. Pathophysiology of Severe Falciparum Malaria
- **PfEMP1 & Cytoadherence:** *P. falciparum* expresses *P. falciparum* erythrocyte membrane protein-1 (PfEMP1) on electron-dense surface knobs on infected erythrocytes. PfEMP1 binds endothelial receptors: **CD36**, **ICAM-1**, and chondroitin sulfate A (CSA).
- **Microvascular Sequestration:** Infected erythrocytes adhere to vascular endothelium and uninfected red cells (rosetting), causing microvascular obstruction in deep vascular beds (cerebral microcirculation, kidneys, placenta).
- **Life-Threatening Manifestations:** Cerebral malaria (diffuse encephalopathy, unarousable coma, retinal hemorrhages), severe lactic acidosis, acute pulmonary edema, acute kidney injury (blackwater fever / massive intravascular hemolysis), and profound hypoglycemia (parasite glucose consumption and quinine-induced hyperinsulinism).
- **Treatment:** Intravenous **Artesunate** (first-line for severe malaria), followed by a complete oral course of Artemisinin-based Combination Therapy (ACT: Artemether-Lumefantrine or Artesunate-Amodiaquine).
