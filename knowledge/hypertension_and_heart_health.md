# Hypertension and Cardiovascular Health Guidelines

## What is Hypertension?
Hypertension (high blood pressure) is a chronic medical condition in which the systemic arterial blood pressure is persistently elevated. Blood pressure is recorded as two numbers:
- **Systolic pressure (top number):** The pressure in blood vessels when the heart contracts or beats.
- **Diastolic pressure (bottom number):** The pressure in the vessels when the heart rests between beats.

Hypertension is diagnosed when measured on two different days is systolic blood pressure on both days is ≥140 mmHg and/or the diastolic blood pressure on both days is ≥90 mmHg according to WHO guidelines (or ≥130/80 mmHg under AHA/ACC guidelines).

## The "Silent Killer"
Hypertension is often referred to as a "silent killer" because the vast majority of people with high blood pressure experience no noticeable warning signs or symptoms. Over time, untreated high blood pressure damages arterial walls, significantly escalating the risk of stroke, myocardial infarction (heart attack), heart failure, kidney disease, and vascular dementia.

## Modifiable Risk Factors and Lifestyle Modifications
The World Health Organization highlights key evidence-based interventions to prevent and control hypertension:
- **Sodium Restriction:** Reduce daily dietary sodium intake to less than 2,000 mg per day (equivalent to about 5 grams or 1 teaspoon of table salt). Avoid heavily processed meats, canned soups, instant noodles, and packaged snack foods.
- **Heart-Healthy Nutrition (DASH Diet):** Emphasize vegetables, fresh fruits, whole grains, legumes, lean poultry, and nuts. Increase dietary potassium (bananas, spinach, sweet potatoes, avocados), which counters sodium's hypertensive effects.
- **Healthy Weight Maintenance:** Losing even 5% to 10% of excess body weight can produce substantial reductions in both systolic and diastolic blood pressure.
- **Regular Aerobic Physical Exercise:** Engage in at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic exercise (e.g., brisk walking, swimming, cycling) per week.
- **Limiting Alcohol and Quitting Tobacco:** Tobacco use causes immediate acute vasoconstriction and chronic atherosclerosis. Alcohol consumption should be moderated or eliminated.
- **Stress Management:** Incorporate relaxation routines and adequate sleep (7–9 hours nightly).

## When to Seek Urgent Clinical Attention
A blood pressure reading exceeding 180/120 mmHg is classified as a **Hypertensive Crisis**. If accompanied by chest pressure, shortness of breath, back pain, numbness, vision changes, or difficulty speaking, emergency medical services must be contacted immediately.
